# Nuance Blocks Library

Pre-approved nuance blocks organized by product cluster. Use during the Accurate Product Update workflow.

---

## How to select and insert a nuance block

**All three conditions must be true before inserting:**
1. The article makes a claim that needs balancing: either strongly positive (add an honest limitation) or addresses scepticism/a pain point (add a positive surprise).
2. The block adds information the article doesn't already contain. If the article already addresses the limitation or capability, the block is redundant.
3. The block matches the specific claim in that section: not just the general topic.

**Placement:** Directly after the paragraph that makes the relevant claim.
**Volume:** Maximum 2 per article. Default is 1. Count existing nuance blocks first (identified by opener patterns: "Worth knowing:", "One honest note:", "A practical note:", "Good to know:", "Something to factor in:", "One thing worth knowing:", "Something worth knowing going in:", "Worth being upfront about:", "Worth highlighting:", "Worth being clear on:", "Genuinely underused:", "Not obvious but genuinely useful:", "More capable than it looks:", "Better than most people expect:", "Stronger than its reputation:", "Faster than most people expect:", "Easier to underestimate than overestimate:", "Broader than most people realise:"). If the article already has 2+, skip.

**Do not standardize openers**: the variety is intentional.

## Website Design

**Template selection**
TYPE: Positive surprise
"Worth knowing: Wix's template library is large, but treat any template as a starting point rather than a finished design. Businesses that spend time customizing fonts, colors and section layouts to match their brand consistently end up with better-performing sites than those who go live with minimal changes."

**Can't switch templates after publishing**
TYPE: Honest limitation
"One honest note: Wix sites can't switch templates after publishing without rebuilding from scratch. This is one of the most commonly cited limitations and it's real. The practical implication: spend time choosing the right template before you build, because changing your mind later means starting over. The upside is that Wix's templates are customizable enough that most businesses never need to switch, but that decision belongs at the start, not after you've built twenty pages."

**Mobile design**
TYPE: Practical context
"Worth knowing: Wix's editor lets you adjust the mobile view independently of the desktop layout. This is worth doing deliberately. Don't assume the desktop design translates automatically. Reviewing and refining the mobile version before launch typically takes an hour and has a significant impact on how the site performs for the majority of visitors."

**What the editor actually lets you control**
TYPE: Positive surprise
"More capable than it looks: Wix's drag-and-drop editor gives finer control over layout than most builders at this level. You can position elements with pixel precision, layer sections, control spacing per breakpoint and apply animations without touching code. Most users discover these controls gradually rather than at the start, so it's worth spending an hour exploring the editor before your first real build."

**Platform lock-in / no site export**
TYPE: Honest limitation
"Something worth knowing going in: Wix sites are hosted on Wix infrastructure and can't be exported to another hosting provider. This is a deliberate architectural choice, not an oversight. For most small and mid-size businesses it's a non-issue; the hosting is reliable, secure and maintained automatically. It becomes relevant if your business grows to a point where custom server-level configuration or a specific enterprise hosting arrangement becomes a requirement. The time to think about that question is before you build, not after."

**Migration from another platform**
TYPE: Practical context
"A practical note: If you're migrating an existing site to Wix, content migration, moving blog posts, product listings and existing SEO metadata, takes longer than the design work. Build that into your timeline. The actual build is fast; the content transfer is where most of the time goes."

## Ecommerce

**What Wix Ecommerce handles well out of the box**
TYPE: Positive surprise
"Better than most people expect: Wix Ecommerce ships with abandoned cart recovery, product reviews, discount codes, subscription products and digital downloads without any third-party apps. The checkout is fast, mobile-optimized and supports dozens of payment methods globally. For a small to mid-sized store launching from scratch, the built-in toolset is more complete than many merchants realise before they start comparing platforms."

**Product variants**
TYPE: Practical context
"One thing worth knowing: If your product catalog includes variants across more than two or three attributes, size, color and material for example, plan for extra setup time to structure those correctly. Most stores are live in a few hours; variant-heavy catalogs are closer to a day."

**Multichannel selling**
TYPE: Positive surprise
"Not obvious but genuinely useful: Wix stores can connect to Facebook Shop and Instagram Shopping so inventory, orders and product listings sync across your site and social storefronts from one dashboard. Most merchants set up the website store first and only discover the social channel integrations later. If social commerce is part of your strategy, it's worth setting up at the same time as the main store rather than retrofitting it later."

**Pricing and plan requirements**
TYPE: Honest limitation
"To set expectations: Wix's Core plan ($29/month) gets you a functional storefront, but several features that growing stores rely on, automated sales tax, advanced shipping, multiple currencies and abandoned cart recovery, require the Business plan ($39/month) or higher. Factor the full plan cost into your budget from the start, not after you hit the feature wall."

**International selling**
TYPE: Practical context
"Something to factor in: If international sales are a meaningful part of your business from launch, verify your specific tax and currency requirements against Wix's current plan features before you build."

**Wholesale or B2B**
TYPE: Honest limitation
"Worth knowing if you're in wholesale or B2B: Wix's default storefront is built for direct-to-consumer. Wholesale pricing tiers and account-based ordering are solvable, but they require additional configuration that standard retail setups don't."

**Dropshipping**
TYPE: Practical context
"Worth knowing: Setting up a dropshipping store on Wix is straightforward, but the platform is only one part of the equation. Supplier reliability, shipping times and return handling sit entirely outside Wix. Those relationships determine the customer experience more than the store setup does."

**Payments and checkout**
TYPE: Practical context
"One thing worth knowing: Wix Payments is the fastest way to get a store accepting payments and it covers most standard needs. If your business requires a specific payment provider, due to region, industry or existing contracts, verify compatibility before you build the checkout flow. Switching payment providers after launch is doable but disruptive."

**Built-in ecommerce tools (abandoned cart, reviews)**
TYPE: Positive surprise
"Worth knowing: Wix's built-in ecommerce tools, abandoned cart recovery, product reviews and discount codes, are active by default but only effective if configured deliberately. Abandoned cart emails in particular are one of the higher-return setups a store can do in the first week. Most store owners enable them and never revisit the defaults; a small amount of customization significantly improves conversion."

## SEO

**What Wix SEO actually includes**
TYPE: Positive surprise
"Stronger than its reputation: Wix's SEO toolset has been rebuilt significantly since the platform's early years. It now includes automatic sitemaps, canonical tags, structured data, server-side rendering, direct Google Search Console integration with a built-in site inspection tool, keyword research powered by Semrush and an AI-assisted SEO setup checklist per page. For most small business websites, the native toolset covers everything needed to rank competitively without any third-party plugins."

**Competitive verticals**
TYPE: Practical context
"Worth knowing: Wix's SEO tools cover everything a standard business site needs, meta titles, structured data, sitemaps and canonical tags. Where you'll want to go deeper is if you're targeting highly competitive national keywords in a saturated vertical. That's less a platform question and more a content and link-building investment that any platform would require."

**Technical SEO limitations**
TYPE: Honest limitation
"One nuance worth understanding: Wix is a closed platform, which means certain advanced technical SEO configurations aren't accessible. No server-level .htaccess control, no custom response headers, structured data has a character limit and you can't configure AI crawling files. For the vast majority of business websites this never becomes a constraint. For technical SEOs who want granular control over every ranking variable, these are real limits worth knowing about upfront."

**Multilingual SEO**
TYPE: Practical context
"A practical note: Wix supports multilingual sites, but there's a known limitation worth planning for: URL slugs for translated pages mirror the original language structure rather than allowing fully customized paths. For most businesses serving multiple languages this is workable. For international SEO strategies that require fully independent URL architectures per language, it's a constraint to factor into your setup plan."

**SEO for large sites**
TYPE: Practical context
"Something to factor in: Wix's SEO tools work well at small and medium site scales. As site size grows, bulk metadata management, URL redirect management at scale and crawl budget optimization become more time-consuming than they would in an open CMS. If you're building a large site from the start, budget extra time for these tasks or explore whether Wix's bulk SEO tools meet your specific needs."

**Results timeline**
TYPE: Practical context
"One nuance here: SEO results from a new site typically take weeks to months to compound, regardless of how well-optimized the content is. The setup work pays off, just not immediately. Businesses that expect organic traffic in the first few days consistently underestimate this timeline."

**Local SEO**
TYPE: Practical context
"Worth knowing: For businesses that serve a specific city or region, local SEO, Google Business Profile, location-specific landing pages and local citations, typically delivers faster results than broad keyword targeting. Wix's SEO tools support all of this, but the strategy needs to be local-first from the start, not retrofitted later."

**Blogging and content SEO**
TYPE: Practical context
"One thing worth knowing: Wix's blog is a fully capable content SEO tool, but publishing frequency matters less than publishing deliberately. One well-researched article targeting a specific search query outperforms ten general posts. Building a content calendar around actual keyword gaps, rather than topics that feel interesting, is what separates blogs that drive traffic from blogs that don't."

## Bookings

**What Wix Bookings automates once it's set up**
TYPE: Positive surprise
"Easy to underestimate: Once Wix Bookings is configured, it handles the entire client-facing flow automatically: availability display, booking confirmation emails, payment collection, calendar sync and reminder sequences. For a solo service provider, this replaces a meaningful amount of back-and-forth admin that would otherwise happen over email or phone. Most users set it up once and find it runs without intervention from that point."

**Multi-location or multi-staff**
TYPE: Practical context
"Worth knowing: Wix Bookings works smoothly for single-location single-staff service businesses from day one. Managing multiple staff members across multiple locations with overlapping availability rules is possible, but the scheduling logic setup takes meaningful time. Factor that in if your business model involves that complexity from launch."

**Calendar and video sync reliability**
TYPE: Practical context
"One thing to plan for: Wix Bookings integrates with Zoom and syncs to external calendars, but these connections need to be tested and maintained after platform updates. If uninterrupted video session links or real-time calendar sync are critical to your client experience, build in time to test these integrations before going live and after any significant platform changes."

**Classes vs. appointments**
TYPE: Practical context
"Worth knowing: Wix Bookings handles both one-on-one appointments and group classes, but they're configured differently. If your business offers both, set up each service type separately from the start. Combining them under a single service type causes scheduling conflicts that are tedious to untangle after clients have already started booking."

**Payment collection at booking**
TYPE: Practical context
"One thing worth knowing: Wix Bookings gives you the option to collect payment at the time of booking or on arrival. For most service businesses, requiring payment upfront reduces no-shows considerably. The default setting isn't always the right one for every business model. It's worth deciding your policy before you go live and configuring the payment step to match it."

**No-show and cancellation policy defaults**
TYPE: Practical context
"A fair heads-up: Wix Bookings has default cancellation and no-show policies, but they're not necessarily the right defaults for your business. Many service providers go live without reviewing these settings and only notice the gap after a client cancels the night before. Set your cancellation window, refund rules and no-show policy before you take your first booking."

## Domains and Hosting

**What's included in hosting by default**
TYPE: Positive surprise
"Better baseline than most people check: Every Wix site gets SSL, global CDN delivery, 99.99% uptime, automatic security updates and DDoS protection included in all paid plans regardless of tier. There's no separate hosting bill, no server maintenance and no security plugin to manage."

**Domain registration timing**
TYPE: Practical context

TYPE: Practical context
"A practical note: After connecting a custom domain, DNS propagation typically takes a few hours and can take up to 48 hours in some cases. **Professional email setup**
TYPE: Practical context
"Worth knowing: A custom domain email address has a measurable effect on how professional the business appears to clients and suppliers. It's worth setting up at the same time as the site, not after, since the email address will appear across all your contact forms and communications from day one."
